The gateway's guardrails check requests and responses inline (see
[Agent-tool safety guardrails](/predictive-guardrails)). This page covers the three things
you configure yourself, in the dashboard under **Settings › Gateway**:

| Tab | What you set |
|---|---|
| **Guardrail policy** | Your own sensitive-data patterns, and your denied-topic and competitor lists |
| **Guardrail integrations** | A guardrail service you already run, called on your key |

Every change is recorded in the control-change log with who made it.

## Your own sensitive-data patterns

Under **Secrets and personal data**, add named regular expressions, for example an
employee ID `EMP-\d{6}`. Each pattern has its own action:

- **Observe**: the match is recorded and the request continues.
- **Redact**: the match is replaced with a redaction marker before the model or your users see it.
- **Block**: the request is refused.

Patterns apply to requests, responses and streamed output. Up to 10 patterns per policy, each at most 256 bytes.

## Denied topics and competitor names

Under **Topic and competitor terms**, add up to 200 terms per list, each at most 100
bytes. Matching is whole-word and case-insensitive; in scripts written without spaces
(Chinese, Japanese, Thai and similar) a term matches anywhere in the text.

- **Denied topics** are observed by default. Set the rule to **Block** to refuse matching requests.
- **Competitor names** in a response are replaced with `[COMPETITOR]`. In a request they are
  recorded, not rewritten or refused.

A scoped rule (for one API key or project) that sets no lists of its own keeps the
workspace lists.

## Bring your own guardrail service

Under **Guardrail integrations**, connect one of:

- Lakera Guard
- OpenAI Moderation
- Azure Content Safety
- Azure Prompt Shields
- AWS Bedrock Guardrails
- Google Cloud Model Armor
- Microsoft Presidio (self-hosted)
- **Your own endpoint**: Tracelane sends each request to your HTTPS endpoint, signed with
  your secret, and follows its allow, deny or redact answer.

Each integration runs on **your** credentials, so the vendor is your processor, not
ours. Up to 8 integrations per workspace. Each integration has its own timeout, up to 5
seconds (the dashboard suggests 2).

### When the service does not answer

Each integration states its failure mode:

- **Closed** (the default): if the service times out or errors, the request is refused.
- **Open**: the request continues and the failure is recorded.

Presidio is closed only. For Model Armor, a service-account key that Google rejects
refuses the request whatever the failure mode, and the rejection is remembered for five
minutes, so later requests are refused at once instead of waiting on the token service.

(an open-source self-host with no Postgres) they resolve to the free tier. See
